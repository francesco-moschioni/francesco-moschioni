A structural account of who can adjust to congestion pricing.

My MSc thesis studies heterogeneous substitution and the demand-side incidence of New York City's 2025 Congestion Relief Zone.

MSc thesis · 2025–2026

Bridge and Tunnel: The Geography of Substitution under New York's Congestion Pricing

I develop a multimodal random-coefficients nested-logit demand model to estimate how travelers substitute across modes and locations. The model combines trip records, network and survey data, travel costs and times, and origin-zone income distributions. Because the sample is almost entirely post-policy, the no-toll regime is evaluated as a structural counterfactual.

Selected findings

Income alone does not order the incidence.

The estimates point to a stronger spatial pattern: access to transport alternatives is central to travelers' capacity to respond to the charge.

6.3%

Manhattan's share of the partial-equilibrium gain, versus 35.4% in Queens and 35.2% in Brooklyn.

Scope. These results characterize demand-side incidence, not the complete welfare incidence of congestion pricing. Revenue recycling and a full transport equilibrium remain outside the counterfactual.

Interpretation

Transport geography is an economic margin.

The capacity to substitute depends importantly on where travelers are located and which alternatives are available to them.

Car own-price elasticity does not move monotonically with income. Spatial heterogeneity is more pronounced and closely associated with subway accessibility, including outside Manhattan. The borough pattern is consistent with that mechanism: Manhattan, with dense subway coverage, receives the smallest share of the gain from removing the charge.

Research design

From fragmented mobility records to a multimodal demand system.

The structural model is portable; the measurement and identification layers depend on the local data environment.

Measure the market

Construct quantities, generalized prices, travel times, availability, market size, and demographics for eight modes.

Reconstruct missing flows

Estimate private-car and bus origin–destination flows with gravity models where direct spatial records are unavailable.

Estimate heterogeneity

Use a random-coefficients nested-logit model with price sensitivity varying across origin-zone income distributions.

Evaluate the counterfactual

Remove mode-specific congestion charges and resolve demand under partial equilibrium and congestion feedback.
